Parking Lot Striping

What Parking Lot Striping Includes

In short: Adams Bros Striping paints and refreshes the lines and markings that organize a parking lot — stall lines, ADA accessible spaces and access aisles, fire lanes, directional arrows, crosswalks, stencils, and curb markings — for commercial properties throughout East Tennessee. Most lots are finished in a single day, and estimates are free.

Striping is the first thing drivers read when they pull into your property. Clear, bright lines tell them where to park, where to walk, and which way to drive. Faded or confusing lines do the opposite: cars park crooked, stalls get wasted, ADA spaces become hard to identify, and the whole property looks neglected.

Andrew and Bradley Adams run every job themselves with professional-grade striping equipment. Whether you need a quick refresh before a busy season or a complete new layout, you're dealing with the owners from the first call to the final walkthrough.

Re-Striping vs. a New Layout

Re-striping repaints your existing layout in the same places. It's the right call when the plan still works but the paint has faded — typically every 1 to 3 years depending on traffic and sun. A new layout makes sense when a lot has been resurfaced or sealcoated to a blank surface, when you want to add stalls or ADA spaces, or when traffic flow isn't working. Fresh sealcoating followed by new striping is the most complete "like new" refresh for a tired lot.

ADA Parking Space Requirements

Accessible parking is required for most businesses open to the public. Under the 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design, the minimum number of accessible spaces depends on the total number of spaces in the lot, and at least one of every six accessible spaces must be van-accessible.

Minimum accessible parking spaces under the 2010 ADA Standards
Total spaces in lotMinimum accessible spaces
1 – 251
26 – 502
51 – 753
76 – 1004
101 – 1505
151 – 2006

Standard accessible spaces are at least 8 feet wide with a 5-foot access aisle; van-accessible spaces need either an 11-foot space with a 5-foot aisle or an 8-foot space with an 8-foot aisle. Accessible spaces should sit on the shortest accessible route to the entrance. Local codes can add requirements, so for new construction or major alterations, confirm with your local building official — we're happy to walk through your lot with you.
